# Paper Explain Concept

Use this skill when a user supplies a paper PDF, accessible full text or link and asks for a structured deep reading. Base the analysis on the material actually available. State whether the source is full text, abstract or an excerpt; do not infer unobserved results or methods. If the source cannot be read, ask for an accessible copy.

## Workflow

1. Identify the paper and source, and explain in two or three plain sentences the problem and what the authors actually did or found.
2. Map the research context using evidence from the paper and verified sources where available. Do not invent a historical timeline or claim priority from the introduction alone.
3. Extract the problem, authors' insight, decisive methods, evidence and results. Distinguish the authors' claims from your interpretation.
4. Explain the contribution compared with the stated baseline, noting the conditions and missing comparisons. Avoid unsupported claims of novelty or superiority.
5. Critically examine assumptions, limitations, alternative explanations and unresolved questions. Label inference and distinguish reported limitations from your own assessment.
6. Draw a compact text diagram: problem → insight → method → evidence → conclusion, marking gaps where evidence is missing.
7. Present a concise reading note using the structure below. Save it or sync it to another service only when requested and configured by the user.

## Output structure

- Paper title, authors, year, source link or DOI, and material actually read.
- Plain-language explanation.
- Research context and prior gap, with sources where available.
- Core problem and insight.
- Methods and decisive experiments or analyses.
- Main results and evidence, with section, figure or table references when available.
- Contribution and comparison with prior work.
- Assumptions, limitations, alternative interpretations and open questions.
- Logic flow diagram and one-sentence takeaway.

Use clear language, explain unfamiliar terms, and identify missing information. An abstract-only analysis is provisional and cannot establish details absent from the abstract. Do not fabricate citations, numerical findings or figure references.
